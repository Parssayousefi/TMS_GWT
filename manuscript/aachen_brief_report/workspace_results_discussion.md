## Results

### Whole brain and within-workspace information

Whole-brain mean Φ increased from 0.02100 nats before stimulation to 0.02279 afterward. The participant-averaged increase was 0.001792 nats (95% CI 0.000515–0.003070), 8.5% of the baseline group mean, with paired Hedges' gz=0.735. Eleven of 15 participant changes were positive (Figure 1A). The uncorrected test gave t(14)=3.009, p=0.00938. The result survived maximum-t correction across five whole-brain information measures (p=0.02393) and across all ten whole-brain/workspace outcomes (p=0.04291; Table 1). Whole-brain ΦR increased by 0.002220 nats but did not survive five-measure correction (p=0.06580). Persistent synergy had a negative point estimate (−0.007612 nats; five-measure p=0.15747).

Restricting both endpoints to the fixed workspace yielded a smaller Φ increase, 0.001299 nats (95% CI −0.000293–0.002892; five-measure maximum-t p=0.28406). Workspace ΦR increased by 0.001981 nats (0.000173–0.003790; uncorrected p=0.03400), but its corrected p value was 0.10883. None of the five within-workspace information measures survived correction. The PC, gateway and broadcaster outcomes were unchanged by the spatial audit because those analyses already used the fixed mask; none supported a corrected workspace-role change (Supplementary Table S1).

### Conditional normalization and spatial decomposition

Normalized whole-brain Φ increased by 0.712 percentile points (95% CI 0.163–1.262). Its maximum-t p value was 0.04828 across the five whole-brain measures and 0.07825 across both spatial scopes. Holm correction across five gave p=0.07360. Within the workspace, normalized Φ increased by 0.447 percentile points (−0.279–1.173; five-measure maximum-t p=0.51947), and ΦR increased by 0.658 (−0.024–1.341; p=0.18823). No normalized within-workspace measure survived correction (Table 2). Separate reference seeds gave the same interpretation (Supplementary Table S4).

Partitioning connections by the fixed mask identified a positive Φ change among pairs outside the workspace: 0.002809 nats (95% CI 0.001198–0.004420). The normalized increase was 1.151 percentile points (0.513–1.790; Figure 2). These effects survived maximum-t correction across all 20 scope/information combinations (raw p=0.00946; normalized p=0.01270), and the 40-endpoint sensitivity including PC/role measures (p=0.03851 and 0.03479). However, the direct outside-minus-within Φ contrast did not survive five-measure correction (raw p=0.16522; normalized p=0.16809). The data therefore do not establish preferential modulation outside the workspace.

### Temporal decomposition and signal representation

The outside-workspace Φ increase did not correspond to an increase in total joint temporal information. At the group-mean level, joint time-delayed mutual information decreased by 0.008918 nats, while the summed within-region self-information decreased by 0.011727 nats. Their difference is the observed +0.002809-nat Φ change. The individual component changes did not survive the nine-test mechanistic correction, so this identity describes the estimated balance rather than establishing a separately significant physiological mechanism.

Restricting every deconvolved scan to 294 samples preserved the outside-workspace Φ estimate (+0.002848 nats). Applying the same Gaussian Φ formula to denoised BOLD before deconvolution gave an outside-workspace estimate of +0.000200 nats (95% CI −0.001794–0.002194; uncorrected p=0.8330), and a whole-brain estimate of −0.000284 nats (p=0.6850). The direct method-by-time difference survived correction for whole-brain Φ (eight-test maximum-t p=0.03607), but not outside-workspace Φ (p=0.07629). The positive finding is therefore sensitive to signal representation and is not reproduced directly in denoised BOLD (Supplementary Table S9).

### Participant influence, intensity and motion

All leave-one-participant-out whole-brain Φ estimates remained positive, ranging from 0.001392 to 0.002030 nats, with uncorrected p<0.021 in each omission (Supplementary Figure S1). The original 10,000-resample bootstrap interval was 0.000762–0.002978 nats and the Wilcoxon p value was 0.00537. In contrast, the workspace Φ bootstrap interval included zero (−0.000123–0.002717), consistent with its uncertain mean effect. These are dependent checks of the same sample.

Whole-brain Φ changes were positive at both intensities: 0.002043 nats at 90% rMT (95% CI −0.000254–0.004341) and 0.001541 at 120% (−0.000624–0.003706). Their paired difference was −0.000503 nats (−0.004164–0.003159). The individual visit changes correlated negatively (r=−0.346), so improved precision after averaging should not be interpreted as independent replication. Neither workspace Φ intensity-specific interval excluded zero; no corrected intensity contrast established a dose difference (Supplementary Table S3).

Mean FD increased from 0.1364 to 0.1415 mm, a difference of 0.0052 mm (95% CI −0.0183–0.0286). Motion-adjusted whole-brain Φ changed by 0.001787 nats, close to the unadjusted estimate, but its five-measure Holm p value was 0.16647. Motion-adjusted workspace Φ and ΦR intervals included zero (nominal p=0.18277 and 0.11110). Outside-workspace Φ retained five-measure Holm support after motion adjustment (raw p=0.03983; normalized p=0.02262), but not correction across all 20 scope/information models (p=0.15930 and 0.09048; Supplementary Table S2).

The outside-workspace increase was positive in 13 of 15 participants in raw units and 12 of 15 after normalization. Every leave-one-participant-out estimate remained positive (raw range 0.002366–0.003095 nats; uncorrected p≤0.00505). Removing each canonical network in turn also retained a positive corrected outside-workspace mean. However, none of the 35 estimable individual network-pair blocks survived correction, precluding a specific network localization. Baseline-plus-motion adjustment retained the outside-workspace estimate, but the adjusted outside-minus-inside contrast still did not survive the six-model sensitivity correction. These findings strengthen the within-sample aggregate result without establishing spatial specificity (Supplementary Table S9).

The inferential scope also mattered without motion adjustment. Treating the ten whole-brain/workspace information outcomes and 20 PC/role outcomes as one 30-endpoint family yielded whole-brain Φ maximum-t p=0.15936 raw and p=0.25073 normalized. Thus the global result was not retained by every correction or sensitivity analysis.

## Discussion

Mean pairwise whole-minus-sum information increased after prefrontal iTBS when the two visits were averaged within participant. The fixed-workspace analysis qualifies that finding: neither within-workspace Φ nor ΦR survived correction across the five information measures. The clearest spatially resolved evidence concerned Φ among non-workspace pairs, but a corrected direct comparison did not establish a larger effect there. These findings support a descriptive information change following active stimulation, rather than a selective enhancement of workspace integration.

### Information measure and spatial scope determine the claim

Φ compares joint temporal information with the sum of each region's own past–future information. It combines several ΦID components and is reduced by persistent redundancy [@mediano2025]. Here, the outside-workspace increase arose because the estimated summed self-information fell more than joint information. This is consistent with a shift in the balance of temporal predictability, not necessarily an absolute increase in information available through regional interactions. It also explains why a Φ increase can coexist with a decrease in persistent synergy. The component-wise uncertainty prevents attributing this balance to a specific neural mechanism.

The same distinction applies to spatial interpretation. A whole-brain mean includes workspace-to-workspace, outside-to-outside and cross-boundary connections. An increase in that mean does not identify the workspace as its source. Although outside-workspace Φ survived broad unadjusted-model correction, the nonsignificant direct spatial comparison prevents a claim of preferential outside-workspace modulation. The mask describes an independently defined reference architecture, not a binary distinction between regions that can and cannot contribute to conscious processing.

Luppi and colleagues used pairwise ΦR networks and network-based correction to identify altered connections, then related those connections to the synergistic workspace [@luppi2024]. Their result is not a scalar average over the mask. Yousefi and colleagues examined workspace participation and gateway/broadcaster organization under psychedelics [@yousefi2026]. Our shared estimator and reference mask facilitate methodological comparison, but do not make the outcomes interchangeable. The absence of corrected role changes here also means that the current findings do not reproduce the psychedelic workspace pattern. Neither Φ nor the present spatial summaries provide a direct measurement of consciousness or subjective experience.

### Robustness and limits

The whole-brain estimate was not driven by a single participant, and adjustment for mean FD barely changed its magnitude. Nevertheless, robust uncertainty and family-wise correction weakened its support. This does not identify measured mean motion as the cause of the effect, but mean FD cannot capture all motion-related contamination [@power2012]. Without sham, repeated scanning, expectancy, arousal, sensory experience and physiological changes remain alternative explanations. Acquisition after, rather than during, stimulation avoids simultaneous pulse artifacts but does not remove these influences.

Conditional normalization asks whether an information measure occupies a different position relative to a matched-total-information null [@liardi2025]. It is not a general guarantee against estimation bias. Reference-seed stability and numerical validation support implementation integrity, while the empirical baseline prior, Gaussian stationary model and short records constrain physiological interpretation. The same baseline-derived reference was retained across scopes, making their comparison consistent, but uncertainty in the learned prior was not propagated. Raw and normalized results are sensitivity analyses of the same observations.

The small sample, absence of sham and exploratory selection of endpoints and spatial follow-ups constrain generalization. Family-wise correction covers the reported sets, not the full sequence of historical analytical decisions. Confidence intervals are not selection-adjusted. Negative correlation of the two visit responses further limits a claim of reproducible individual modulation; nonsignificant intensity differences do not establish equivalence. Additional limitations include one parcellation, lag and preprocessing chain, exclusion for inconsistent slice-timing metadata, and no susceptibility-distortion correction. Haemodynamic deconvolution does not make fMRI a direct neuronal measurement.

The absence of a comparable Φ increase before deconvolution is a material limitation. Deconvolution changes temporal dependencies by design and could reveal dynamics obscured by haemodynamic smoothing, but estimated response functions could also influence the effect. Neither the nine HRF-parameter contrasts nor six motion-adjusted HRF–Φ associations survived correction (Supplementary Table S10); these negative checks do not validate a neural interpretation. An independent dataset and comparison of validated deconvolution methods would be needed to distinguish these explanations.

The original study reported intensity-dependent frontostriatal functional connectivity changes [@alkhasli2019]. Another reanalysis of the same dataset reported intensity-dependent striatal brain-entropy changes [@ben2025]. These findings motivate a distributed, temporally structured response to prefrontal stimulation, but are not independent replications of the present result. Our analysis does not establish superior sensitivity, reliability or clinical value. Healthy participants and absent subjective or therapeutic endpoints preclude claims about antidepressant benefit or the phenomenology of stimulation. Comparisons with psychedelic fMRI and electrophysiology remain conceptual [@yousefi2026; @blackburne2026].

The contribution is therefore a distinction relevant to neuromodulation research: a global pairwise Φ increase can be observed without corrected evidence for increased information integration within a fixed global workspace. Reporting the measure, spatial aggregation and inferential scope together makes that result interpretable and testable in independent sham-controlled data.
